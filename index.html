The company

MiniMines is a Bengaluru clean-tech company in the KIADB Industrial Area, Doddaballapur. It recycles lithium-ion batteries and e-waste and recovers lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese using its proprietary Hybrid-Hydrometallurgy (HHM™) process. Its Giga Mines facility (₹350 crore, with Government of Karnataka support) is the next stage of that work.

The design problem

The company does serious chemistry, compliance and manufacturing work, but the current site reads as a small, unfinished brochure. The redesign has to look like a credible manufacturer that a global OEM, an EPR-obligated producer or a journalist can trust, and give each of them one clear next step.

1. Method & evidence basis

This is a desk-based design review. It uses what the assignment gives and what can be checked in the redesigned build. It is not user research, and it says so.

Sources used

The MiniMines assignment brief, including its own audit points, content inventory and audience table. The five benchmark sites it names. The redesigned build (navigation, home page and Battery Recycling page, design tokens).

What was not done

No user interviews, analytics, heat-maps or usability tests. The live site was not re-audited screen by screen, so the audit relies on the brief’s findings. Mobile issues below are risks to test, not observed failures.

How to read the tags

Observed stated in the brief. Inferred follows from the brief or the code. Assumed needs checking with MiniMines.

How I would validate

Five-second test of the new hero. Task test: “send batteries for recycling” and “request EPR support”, on phone and desktop. Review of enquiry sources and bounce rates. Interviews with two EPR producers.

6. Moodboard & visual direction

Direction in four words: credible, precise, calm, industrial-clean. Real materials and labs, not stock clichés of leaves and light bulbs. Photos below are the prototype’s illustrative images.

Colour

Deep blues for authority, Sky as the only accent, plenty of white. No green “eco” palette.

Type

Large, tight Bricolage Grotesque headlines with plain Inter body text.

Imagery

Real plant, lab and material photos, cropped large with rounded corners. Captions mark illustrative images.

Avoid

Stock leaves and globes, neon gradients, dense charts, and claims without a source.

7. Design rationale

Four solutions, one hero

The hero states the mission and offers two actions. A strip beneath links to the four solutions, so each visitor finds their route in one tap. Answers pins N, 2.

A home page that is a story

What MiniMines does, how (HHM™), why it is better, who stands behind it. Detail lives on the Battery Recycling page. Answers the twelve-section scroll.

Trust is shown, not claimed

Partners, awards, accreditations and press sit together as one evidence block, with every figure sourced or removed. Answers pins 3, 9, 10.

One enquiry system

A single dialog replaces three look-alike forms, and the same label always leads to the same place. Answers pin C.

Mobile first, accessible by default

Targets of 44 px or more, WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, visible focus, tables that scroll inside their own box.

Giga Mines in context

A banner and a home-page block link to its story without competing with the core offer.

Lucide icons, 24 px grid, 2 px round stroke, drawn in the current text colour. Sizes: 16 inside buttons, 20 in lists, 24 standalone, 32 in feature cards. Decorative icons are hidden from screen readers; an icon-only button always has an accessible name and a 44 px target.

12. Rules and accessibility

  • WCAG 2.2 AA. Text 4.5:1, large text and controls 3:1, targets at least 44 × 44 px.
  • Visible focus. 3 px outline, Brand on light, Sky on dark, 3 px offset. Never removed.
  • Mobile first. Design at 390 px before 1440 px; nothing scrolls sideways except tables in their own container.
  • One primary action per view. A call to action names what happens and always goes to the same place.
  • Forms. Real labels, helper text linked with aria-describedby, aria-invalid on errors, and a summary with role="alert".
  • Images. Descriptive alt text; decorative images have empty alt. Captions mark illustrative photos.
  • Honest content. Figures are sourced or marked [TBC].
  • Tokens only. Use named colours, the type scale and the spacing scale; no one-off values.
